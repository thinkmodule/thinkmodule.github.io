06 • Mathematical Foundations Behind PyTorch Optimizers Why Second-Order Geometry Dictates Training Speed

Hessian Curvature & Stability Bound

H = ∇²L(θ),   ηmax,SGD < 2 / λmax(H)

In narrow ravines (κ = λmax/λmin ≫ 1), vanilla SGD must keep η tiny to avoid exploding on steep walls, causing agonizingly slow progress along the valley floor.

AdamW Decoupled Adaptive Update

θt+1 = θt − η · m̂t / (√v̂t + ε) − ηλθt

Dividing by √v̂t acts as a diagonal approximation of the inverse Hessian curvature, rescaling steep and flat axes to equalize step sizes — escaping saddles and ravines effortlessly.

Saddle Points in High Dimensions

λ&sub1; > 0,   λ&sub2; < 0  ⇒  det(H) < 0 (Indefinite)

In deep neural networks, most critical points where ∇L ≈ 0 are saddle points, not local minima. Adaptive normalization amplifies tiny escape gradients along negative-curvature directions.
